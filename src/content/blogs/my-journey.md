## ⚡ I Don't Have Everything Figured Out

I don't.

And that's exactly why I'm writing.

My journey so far hasn't been a straight line where every decision was obvious and every outcome was planned.

There were things I understood early.

Things I understood much later.

Decisions that worked.

Decisions I would make differently today.

Moments where I knew exactly what I wanted.

And moments where I had no idea what the next step should be.

I want to document all of it.

Not just the destination.

>The reasoning behind the journey.

---

## 🧭 My Journey Is Made of Decisions

When I look back, I don't see a perfect roadmap.

I see a series of choices.

```
                     MY JOURNEY

        ┌───────────────┐
        │   Experience  │
        └───────┬───────┘
                │
                ▼
        ┌───────────────┐
        │    Decision   │
        └───────┬───────┘
                │
        ┌───────┴────────┐
        ▼                ▼
     Worked           Didn't Work
        │                │
        ▼                ▼
     Growth           Learning
        │                │
        └────────┬───────┘
                 ▼
             Next Step
```

Every stage taught me something.

Sometimes the lesson came from success.

More often, it came from realizing that something wasn't working the way I expected.

That's why I don't want to record only **what happened**.

I want to record **why it happened**.

---

## 🔍 What People See vs. What Actually Happened

From the outside, a journey can look very simple.

| What people see |
| :--- |
| Learn → Work → Gain Experience → Grow |

But the real version is closer to:

```
Learn
  ↓
Question
  ↓
Try
  ↓
Struggle
  ↓
Make a decision
  ↓
Get it wrong
  ↓
Understand why
  ↓
Try again
  ↓
Improve
```

The second version is the one I'm interested in.

Because the difficult parts usually contain the most valuable lessons.

A resume can tell you **what I did**.

A blog can explain **why I did it**.

---

## 🧠 Every Choice Has a Story

Whenever I make an important decision, there is usually more behind it than what people see.

Why did I choose one direction instead of another?

Why did I start learning something new?

Why did I change my approach?

Why did I continue when something became difficult?

Why did I believe something at that point in my journey?

And perhaps the most important question:

> Would I make the same decision today?

Sometimes the answer is yes.

Sometimes it isn't.

And that's okay.

Because growth isn't about proving that my past decisions were always correct.

It's about understanding them better.

---

## 🔄 The Person Making the Decision Also Changes

One thing I've learned is that decisions cannot always be judged using today's knowledge.

The person I was yesterday didn't have today's experience.

The information I have today wasn't available to me then.

So instead of asking:

> "Why didn't I know this earlier?"

I try to ask:

> "What did I know at that time, and why did that decision make sense to me?"

That question changes the way I look at my own mistakes.

It turns regret into reflection.

And reflection into learning.

---

## 📖 Why I Write Instead of Just Remembering

Because memory keeps the result.

Writing keeps the reasoning.

I might remember that something worked.

But I may forget:

| What I may forget |
| :--- |
| what I was struggling with |
| what alternatives I considered |
| what I was afraid of |
| what information influenced the decision |
| what I learned afterward |
| what I would do differently today |

Writing forces me to slow down.

To put scattered thoughts into order.

To separate what I **thought** from what actually **happened**.

And sometimes, while writing about an old experience, I understand it differently for the first time.

That's one of the biggest reasons I write.

---

## 🪞 Writing Is Also a Mirror

This blog isn't only for someone else to read.

It's for me.

When I write something down, I can see my own thinking from the outside.

Sometimes I notice:

> "I was overthinking this."

Sometimes:

> "I should have asked earlier."

Sometimes:

> "That decision actually taught me more than I realized."

And sometimes:

> "I still don't have an answer."

That's fine too.

Not every post needs to end with a perfect conclusion.

Sometimes the honest conclusion is:

> I'm still figuring this out.

---

## 🌱 Growth Isn't Always an Achievement

I've started to see growth differently.

Growth isn't always:

| Growth isn't always |
| :--- |
| New Job |
| New Skill |
| New Project |
| New Title |

Sometimes it's much smaller.

```
Understanding something
        ↓
Making fewer mistakes
        ↓
Asking better questions
        ↓
Thinking more clearly
        ↓
Making better decisions
```

Some of the most meaningful changes don't appear on a resume.

They happen in the way you think.

The way you respond to problems.

The way you handle uncertainty.

The way you recover when something doesn't go according to plan.

Those changes deserve to be remembered too.

| Things I'm learning |

| There will also be posts about |
| :--- |

| Learning |

| Mistakes |

| Changing perspectives |

I'm also trying to understand **the person behind the engineer**.

---

## 🧩 What You'll Find Here

I want every post to have a reason for existing.

Not:

> "Here is another thing I know."

But:

> "Here is something I experienced, thought about, and learned from."

A post might start with:

```
Something happened
        ↓
I had to make a choice
        ↓
I chose a direction
        ↓
Something changed
        ↓
I learned something
        ↓
I'm sharing the reasoning
```

Sometimes there will be a clear lesson.

Sometimes there will be several.

And sometimes the lesson might simply be that I still have more to learn.

---

## 🚫 This Isn't a Success Story

At least, I don't want to present it that way.

I'm still in the middle of the journey.

There are things I'm proud of.

There are things I wish I had handled differently.

There are goals I'm still chasing.

There are skills I'm still developing.

There are questions I still don't have answers to.

And there will probably be more uncertainty ahead.

That's not a problem.

That's the journey.

I don't want to wait until everything is perfect before documenting it.

Because if I do, I'll only document the polished version.

And the polished version isn't the whole story.

---

## 🕰️ I Want Future Me to Read This

This might actually be the biggest reason I write.

I want to be able to come back years from now and see:

```
What I believed
       ↓
What I experienced
       ↓
What changed
       ↓
What I learned
       ↓
Who I became
```

Maybe I'll agree with what I wrote.

Maybe I'll disagree completely.

I hope I disagree with some of it.

Because that would mean I've grown.

These posts are snapshots.

Not final answers.

---

## ✍️ So, Why Do I Write?

Not because I have everything figured out.

Not because I have all the answers.

Not because every decision I've made was right.

I write because **my journey has a story behind it**.

And I don't want to remember only the results.

I want to remember the questions.

The decisions.

The uncertainty.

The mistakes.

The small wins.

The changes in direction.

The lessons.

And the person I was while going through all of them.

>I write to look back, understand better, and keep moving forward.

If something I write helps someone else along the way, that's a bonus.

But first, this blog is a record for me.

**A record of how I figured things out.**
